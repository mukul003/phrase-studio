# Topic-only conversational script prompt

Change only the topic on the first line. The live app fills it automatically. Labels organize the recording; the spoken writing should flow naturally.

```text
TOPIC: [REPLACE ONLY THIS TOPIC]

Write a warm, natural English-speaking practice episode for Fluent English For You. It will be read aloud by ONE friendly narrator to ONE adult listener. The listener is a beginner. The writing should be pleasant to hear: relaxed, clear, connected, and easy to say in one breath at a time.

Make it sound like a friend helping someone find the words for real life. Build the ten phrases around a believable situation or a gentle progression through the topic. Let each moment lead naturally into the next. For a work topic, we might move from arriving at work to asking for help, finishing a task, and going home. Choose a progression that suits the supplied topic.

How to write the spoken parts:
- Intro: open with a small, recognizable moment. Get into the topic quickly and invite the listener along. Keep the warmth understated and personal to the listener.
- Sentence: give one genuinely useful, easy-to-say phrase. Write it once; the recorder controls repetitions.
- Summary: talk about the little moment in which someone would say this. Make the meaning clear through that situation. Use a brief everyday explanation only where it helps. Vary how these passages begin and how long they are.
- Example: let us hear the phrase doing a real job in that situation. A short setup and a quoted line usually work well. The example should add context, a reply, or a small detail that makes the sentence feel real. Keep the phrase and the example logically consistent. Write the example once; the recorder controls repetitions.
- Cue: invite the listener to say the target sentence once. Keep it short, friendly, and connected to the moment. For example, ask them to say it as if they were speaking to a teammate. Keep the task as repeating the sentence, not answering a new question. Vary the wording without making every invitation clever or elaborate.
- Outro: return briefly to the situation or an earlier phrase, then close naturally. Give the listener one small way to use the language today.

Voice and rhythm:
Use contractions and familiar words. Mix short sentences with a few gently flowing ones. Read punctuation as natural phrasing. Let a little warmth or gentle humor come from the situation when it fits. Use an occasional listener-directed aside where it feels natural. Keep the narrator sincere; do not invent personal memories or experiences.

Avoid the repetitive textbook pattern of starting every section with "This means...", "Use this when...", "For example...", and "Now, your turn." Do not replace it with another repeated template such as ten rhetorical questions or ten "Imagine..." openings. Avoid forced laughter, scripted "haha", filler words added for effect, excessive exclamation marks, constant praise, motivational slogans, and dramatic or poetic wording. Simple English can still have a natural adult voice.

STYLE REFERENCE ONLY — use this level of warmth and specificity, without copying it into an unrelated topic:
Sentence: I need a little more time.
Summary: You’re nearly finished, but there’s one thing left to check. It’s okay to ask for a few more minutes.
Example: A teammate asks if the report is ready. You can say, "I need a little more time. Can I send it after lunch?"
Cue: Say that as if you were talking to someone at your desk.

Before returning the script, silently read the spoken text aloud in your head. Smooth out stiff wording, repeated openings, awkward transitions, and examples that merely repeat the definition. Keep the pace unhurried without padding the lesson to reach an exact duration. Aim for a compact episode of roughly five minutes including the viewer speaking pauses.

OUTPUT FORMAT FOR PHRASE STUDIO:
Return only the complete script using the labels below. Include exactly 10 numbered Sentence blocks, each followed by Summary, Example, and Cue. These labels are recording metadata and must not be spoken. Put every field on its own line; let longer fields be natural connected paragraphs. Keep transitions inside those fields.

Title: [a short title about the topic]
Intro: [the complete friendly opening]

Sentence 1: [the first target sentence]
Summary: [a brief, natural situation and explanation]
Example: [a believable spoken example]
Cue: [a short invitation to repeat that sentence]

Continue with Sentence 2 through Sentence 10 in the same labeled format, with fresh situations and varied phrasing. Replace every placeholder with finished spoken text.

Outro: [the complete natural closing]

Do not include the style reference, notes about writing, stage directions, speaker labels, timestamps, pause instructions, countdowns, sound effects, Markdown, tables, alternative versions, or commentary outside the script. The recorder controls repetitions and the app inserts the silent viewer turns.
```
